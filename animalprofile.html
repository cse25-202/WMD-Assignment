Animal Profiles

Explore the diet, habitat, and behaviours of Africa's most iconic wildlife.

Diet

CARNIVORE

Lions are apex predators that primarily hunt large ungulates — zebra, wildebeest, and buffalo — cooperating in coordinated group hunts led by females.

Habitat

SAVANNA & GRASSLAND

Lions thrive in open grasslands, savannas, and woodlands. They prefer areas with enough cover for stalking prey and proximity to permanent water sources.

Behaviours

BEHAVIOURAL TRAITS

Social Behaviour

Lions are the only truly social big cats, living in prides of related females, their cubs, and a small coalition of resident males.

Communication

Prides roar to signal territorial boundaries and use scent marking and body language to maintain complex social bonds across their range.

Activity Patterns

Lions are crepuscular — most active at dawn and dusk — and may rest for up to 20 hours per day to conserve energy in the heat.

Diet

HERBIVORE

Elephants are bulk herbivores consuming up to 136 kg of vegetation daily — grasses, leaves, bark, and fruit — spending up to 18 hours feeding.

Habitat

DIVERSE LANDSCAPES

African elephants inhabit diverse ecosystems — from the Chobe floodplains of Botswana to dense woodland and arid scrubland — always near water.

Behaviours

BEHAVIOURAL TRAITS

Social Behaviour

Elephants live in matriarchal herds led by the oldest female. Family bonds are intense — herds cooperate in raising calves and defending against predators.

Communication

Elephants communicate through low-frequency infrasound rumbles that travel kilometres, as well as touch, visual signals, and chemical cues.

Activity Patterns

Largely diurnal but also active at night, elephants migrate seasonally following rainfall and vegetation, covering vast distances across borders.

Diet

OPPORTUNISTIC CARNIVORE

Leopards are versatile hunters preying on over 90 recorded species — from small rodents to medium-sized antelope — and hoist kills into trees to avoid scavengers.

Habitat

ADAPTABLE TERRAIN

The most habitat-adaptable big cat, leopards thrive in rainforest, thornbush, rocky outcrops, and semi-arid savanna from the Cape to the Sahara.

Behaviours

BEHAVIOURAL TRAITS

Social Behaviour

Fiercely solitary and territorial, leopards overlap home ranges only briefly to mate. Females raise cubs alone for up to two years.

Communication

Leopards communicate through rasping cough-like calls, scent deposits on trees, and scratch marks to advertise territory and reproductive status.

Activity Patterns

Primarily nocturnal and crepuscular, leopards rest in dense vegetation or trees during the heat of the day, making them exceptionally difficult to spot.

Diet

BULK GRAZER

Buffalo are non-selective bulk grazers consuming coarse, tall grasses that other ungulates avoid — a critical ecological role in managing savanna vegetation.

Habitat

FLOODPLAINS & WOODLAND

Buffalo depend on access to permanent water and nutritious grasses, inhabiting floodplains, riverine forest, and mixed bushveld across sub-Saharan Africa.

Behaviours

BEHAVIOURAL TRAITS

Social Behaviour

Buffalo form massive herds of hundreds to thousands, displaying remarkable collective decision-making — the herd "votes" on direction of travel by standing and gazing.

Communication

Buffalo communicate using a range of bellows, grunts, and body postures. Alarm calls trigger rapid defensive formations to protect calves from predators.

Activity Patterns

Mainly crepuscular grazers, buffalo wallow in mud and water during the heat of the day — a behaviour that regulates body temperature and removes parasites.

Diet

BROWSER

The black rhino is a selective browser, using its hooked prehensile upper lip to grasp leaves, shoots, and branches from thorny bushes and trees.

Habitat

BUSHVELD & THORNBUSH

Black rhinos favour dense thornbush, semi-arid scrubland, and rocky hillsides. Botswana's dedicated sanctuaries provide secure habitats for reintroduced populations.

Behaviours

BEHAVIOURAL TRAITS

Social Behaviour

Black rhinos are largely solitary and territorial. Mothers and calves form the only lasting bond, with calves remaining dependent for two to three years.

Communication

Rhinos rely heavily on scent — using dung middens and urine spraying to communicate territory, sex, and reproductive state to other individuals.

Activity Patterns

Rhinos are most active at night and during cooler morning and evening hours, spending midday wallowing in mud holes to stay cool and protect against insects.
