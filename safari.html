When to Go

Best Time to Visit Botswana

Botswana has two distinct seasons, each offering a unique safari experience.

Dry Season

Peak
Months
May to October
Temperature
Cool and dry, 6–25 °C
Advantages
Peak wildlife viewing as animals gather at water sources; mokoro excursions fully operational; easier spotting with sparse vegetation.
Considerations
Highest prices of the year; very cold morning drives; book 9–12 months in advance.

Green Season

Off-Peak
Months
November to April
Temperature
Hot and humid, 22–34 °C with thunderstorms
Advantages
Lush landscapes; calving season; migratory birds peak; rates up to 40% cheaper.
Considerations
Some roads may flood; higher mosquito activity; limited water-based activities.

Stay Healthy

Health & Vaccinations

Consult a travel health clinic at least 6–8 weeks before departure.

Malaria

Botswana is a malaria-risk country. Take prophylactics and use DEET repellent consistently.

Recommended Vaccines

Hepatitis A & B, Typhoid, Yellow Fever (if from endemic area), Tetanus, and Rabies.

Water Safety

Drink only bottled or purified water. Avoid ice cubes at camps unless confirmed safe.

Heat & Hydration

Drink 3–4 litres daily. Wear light layers and avoid prolonged midday sun exposure.

In the Field

Safari Safety Rules

Your guide's word is law. These rules exist to protect you and the animals.

Vehicle Safety
  • Stay inside the vehicle unless your guide confirms it is safe
  • Never stand up or lean out while animals are nearby
  • Keep noise to a minimum on all game drives
  • Follow your guide's instructions immediately
  • No sudden movements that might provoke animals
  • Switch off phone ringers on all game drives
Camp Safety
  • Never walk alone after dark — always request an escort
  • Keep tent zipped at all times
  • Do not leave food or scented items in your tent
  • Use provided walkways and designated areas only
  • Keep a flashlight with you at all times
  • Never approach animals, even if they appear tame
Wildlife Encounters
  • Maintain safe distance — never touch or feed animals
  • No flash photography under any circumstances
  • Stay calm if an animal approaches — let it pass
  • Never run from a predator — freeze and stay calm
  • Extra caution around mothers with young
  • Buffalo and hippos are extremely dangerous
Photography Etiquette
  • Respect other guests — don't block views or make noise
  • No flash photography — disturbs wildlife
  • Patience is key — wait for the natural moment
  • Never ask your guide to provoke animals for a shot
  • Never post GPS coordinates of rhino sightings publicly
  • Drone use requires a permit and guide approval

Pro Tip: Book a Licensed Guide

Always use a Professional Guide registered with the Botswana Tourism Organisation (BTO). They know animal behaviour, terrain, and emergency protocols — a great guide transforms a good safari into a life-changing one.
